# IRON NEVER CHANGES — Shirt Concepts

The angle: **real life, not highlight reels.** Men who work, who have people depending
on them, who've had a rough stretch — and who still find 45 minutes. Nothing about
being the strongest guy in the room. Everything about being the guy who came back.

The name already does the heavy lifting. *Iron never changes* — the weight is the one
honest thing in your day. It doesn't care how work went. It gives you exactly what you
put in. That's the whole brand in three words, and the best shirt in the line just
finishes the sentence.

## The shirts themselves — what Bucked Up makes

**Important:** Bucked Up sells its own finished branded apparel. They don't sell blanks
and they don't print other companies' logos. So you can't put your logo on a Bucked Up
shirt — but their heavyweight line is a good reference for the feel you're after, and
there are blanks that match it.

### Bucked Up's thick-material shirts

These three are the heavyweight ones (all 100% cotton, relaxed/boxy "pump cover" cut):

| Shirt | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Heavyweight Pump Cover T-Shirt** | $39.99 | Explicitly "heavyweight 100% cotton," oversized/relaxed |
| **Boxy Pump Cover** | $47.99 | Heavyweight cotton, structured boxy silhouette that sits off the body |
| **Extended Pump Cover** | $47.99 | Same fabric as the Boxy, cut 2" longer |

Their other tees are lighter: the **Premium Core Tee** ($49.99) is described as soft
with "structure" but not heavyweight, the **Gym Strength Tee** ($49.99) is explicitly
"soft, lightweight," and the **Vintage Street Tee** ($44.99) is a washed oversized tee
with no stated weight. Bucked Up doesn't publish oz or GSM numbers on any of them.

### What to actually print your logo on

For the black/white/grey vintage direction, these blanks give you the same thick,
sits-off-the-body feel and take a screen print beautifully:

| Blank | Weight | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Comfort Colors 1717** | 6.1 oz, 100% ring-spun cotton, garment-dyed | The industry standard for the worn-in look. Garment dye means the color has already lived a little. Great in Black, Graphite, Pepper. Widely stocked, easy for any printer to source. |
| **Shaka Wear Max Heavyweight (garment dye)** | 7.5 oz | Marketed as the heaviest garment-dyed tee out there. Boxy/oversized, pigment-and-reactive washed for a faded finish, shrink-resistant. Closest match to a true pump cover. Cheap in bulk. |
| **AS Colour 5082 Heavy Faded Tee** | Heavy, relaxed fit | Premium feel, faded garment-dye colorways, the cleanest fit of the three. Costs more per unit. |
| **Gildan Hammer H000** | 6 oz | Budget heavyweight if you need volume for member giveaways. |

**Ask any printer you call three things:** the oz weight, whether it's garment-dyed
(that's what gives you the vintage color), and whether it's a boxy/relaxed cut. If they
say "6 oz or heavier, garment dyed, relaxed," you're in the right place.

### Print method

Go **screen print** on these, not DTG. One color — black on light shirts, white on
dark. It's cheaper at volume, it lasts longer, and a screen-printed white on a
garment-dyed black tee cracks and fades over time in exactly the way that makes the
vintage set in `06_VINTAGE-DISTRESSED` look right. Send them the SVG files.

## A note on tone

Keep the copy honest and keep it warm underneath the hard edges. The lines that sell
are the ones that say *I know what your week looked like* — not the ones that call
somebody weak for missing a session. "I didn't feel like it either" outsells "no
excuses" every time, because one of them is standing next to you and the other one is
yelling at you.
